## What a living requires from these businesses

To make the comparisons concrete, the examples use **€5,000 monthly operating surplus before your personal tax, YEL and the cost of your own labour**, after the stated business expenses. This is an illustrative business benchmark, not a claim about your household needs or guaranteed take-home pay. The companion dashboard also shows €3,000 and €7,000 scenarios. First-month revenue and one-off deposits must not be mistaken for recurring income.

Most SaaS and service examples below are exclusive of VAT. A Finnish consumer software subscription at €29 including standard VAT leaves about €23.11 before payment processing and service costs. Finland's standard VAT rate is 25.5%; different products and cross-border transactions can receive different treatment. [Finnish Tax Administration](https://www.vero.fi/en/businesses-and-corporations/taxes-and-charges/vat/rates-of-vat/)

YEL is also a separate cost where applicable: the published 2026 contribution rate is 24.4% of confirmed YEL income, not automatically 24.4% of revenue or accounting profit. I have not calculated your personal taxation or assumed a particular YEL assessment. [Varma: YEL in 2026](https://www.varma.fi/en/self-employed/information-for-the-self-employed/yel-in-2026/)

The practical advantage of a focused B2B offer is that a dozen meaningful accounts can matter. The practical disadvantage is that each must have a real reason to pay several hundred euros. A €499 price label is not evidence that buyers exist. Low-priced consumer subscriptions require many more customers and a repeatable acquisition channel. Publishing and games exchange customer sales work for audience and platform dependence; domain trading adds illiquid capital; resale adds physical labour.

## 1. Workforge — strongest overall focus; strongest SaaS candidate

**What you have.** This is considerably more than a job scraper. The code supports a Nordic job catalogue, per-person accounts and documents, saved searches, application tracking, asynchronous CV/cover-letter generation, template-preserving document output, listing analysis and Google Drive synchronization. The current prompt work explicitly attempts to preserve factual candidate information. That is a useful production workflow, although prompt instructions do not prove that generated CVs never invent facts.

Evidence: [application routes and ownership filters](C:/Dev/workforge/apps/api/app/routers/applications.py:455), [user and application data model](C:/Dev/workforge/packages/shared/workforge_shared/models.py:297), [generation grounding instructions](C:/Dev/workforge/apps/worker/worker/generate_prompts.py:14), [Google Drive workflow](C:/Dev/workforge/README.md:89), [application API tests](C:/Dev/workforge/apps/api/tests/test_applications_api.py:51). The README's earlier “not wired for AI yet” wording is superseded by the current generation implementation.

**Using it yourself.** It can make finding and applying for suitable work more efficient. Employment or contract income is income from your qualifications and delivery, not revenue generated by Workforge; it is therefore excluded from the business comparison. The more relevant operating business is using Workforge to prepare reviewed application packs for an established coach's clients. The coach retains career judgment and client relationships; you provide document production and workflow support. This fits the existing application much better than starting a recruitment agency or selling automated employer screening.

**Best paying customer.** Start with owner-led coaching or outplacement firms processing many applications, initially with a Finland/Sweden angle. Sell preservation of existing CV formats, reliable local-language output, source-grounded tailoring and less staff preparation time. Avoid “AI resume builder” as the whole pitch. A private firm that can decide directly is a better initial buyer than a university, municipality or large employer requiring procurement. This is a sales-cycle judgment, not proof that private firms will buy.

**B2C assessment.** Viable category, difficult initial differentiation. Teal already offers a substantial free product and a $29/30-day paid plan. Huntr advertises $40/month with resume tailoring and application packets. These establish competition and price anchors, not Workforge demand. A €19–29 consumer offer needs a specific group and distribution channel, for example a coaching partner serving internationally experienced professionals seeking Finnish/Swedish roles. Jobseekers often stop needing the product after finding work, making churn structural. [Teal pricing](https://www.tealhq.com/pricing), [Huntr pricing](https://api.huntr.co/pricing)

**B2B assessment.** Best of these five for an ongoing software subscription, but coaches already have alternatives: Jobscan has a coach product with client accounts and usage visibility. Your proposed coach workspace is a product extension, not something the current individual-user model already delivers. [Jobscan Coach](https://www.jobscan.co/coach)

Test €149–249/month for a small coaching practice and €399–599/month for a team or assisted package with substantial volume. These are proposed prices. For a €499 package to make sense, demonstrate savings worth substantially more than €499: for example 30 client workflows × 45 minutes saved × €40/hour = €900 of staff time per month. Those inputs must be measured with a customer. If the buyer only needs a few generated paragraphs, the free and cheaper alternatives win.

**Economics.** At €499/account/month and an illustrative €50/account of variable service costs, 13 accounts produce €6,487 revenue and €5,337 operating surplus after another €500 of fixed costs. Costs include an allowance for models, storage, payment fees and support expenses; your own time is additional. If willingness to pay is only €199 with €25 variable cost, reaching the same benchmark needs 32 accounts. Small price changes materially alter the sales workload.

For B2C at €29 VAT-inclusive, assuming €2.50 variable cost and €500 fixed monthly cost, the €5,000 surplus benchmark needs about **267 active subscribers**. At an illustrative 20% monthly churn, around 53–54 replacements are needed every month just to stand still. Neither churn nor these costs were measured in your app.

**What must be finished for the offer.** Coach/client delegation and consent, client separation, review and approval, usage limits tied to paid plans, account deletion/export, clear retention rules, reliable document output, backups and a minimal paid onboarding flow. Individual-user authentication and ownership checks exist; they are not the same as organization permissions. Limit the first product to document workflows buyers actually use.

**Critical platform issue.** The logged-in LinkedIn session scraping is an unsuitable foundation for a dependable commercial service. LinkedIn explicitly prohibits third-party scraping and automation. Workforge can still have value with pasted job descriptions, employer-authorized sources and approved feeds. Job Market Finland documents official job-posting interfaces, but their access terms and onboarding must be satisfied; the present scraper should not be represented as an approved integration. [LinkedIn policy](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387/prohibited-software-and-extensions), [Job Market Finland APIs](https://tyomarkkinatori.fi/en/instructions-and-support/interfaces)

**Commercial gate.** Within four weeks, obtain three paid pilots and compare staff review time against their present process. Aim for at least 30% less preparation time without new factual mistakes. By weeks 6–8, at least two should renew or expand at the intended ongoing price. These are decision rules, not market benchmarks. If buyers will not pay after seeing their own documents improved, more catalogue filters will not fix that.

**Verdict:** primary focus. A good living by month four is plausible through a strong assisted B2B offer, but unproven. A broad self-serve consumer launch is a substantially weaker route for the deadline.

## 2. Playforge — best scalable own-output experiment; strong service alternative

**What you have.** A local HTML5 game production studio with licensed-source ingestion, an immutable original vault, separate gameplay forks, image generation and approval, reproducible builds and Playgama export checks. Its design explicitly targets your own studio. It does not currently provide hosted coding-agent execution or the multi-user isolation that a game-building SaaS would need.

Evidence: [studio design](C:/Dev/playforge/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-playforge-design.md:8), [game/asset data model](C:/Dev/playforge/packages/core/src/db/schema.ts:35), [ship-ready gate](C:/Dev/playforge/packages/core/src/ship-ready.ts:29), [local API authentication](C:/Dev/playforge/apps/api/src/server.ts:37), [technical checklist implementation](C:/Dev/playforge/packages/core/src/playgama-check.ts:108).

**Using it yourself.** Make genuinely enjoyable games, distribute through portals and earn a share of advertising or purchase revenue. Unlike physical resale, a successful game can serve more players with limited fulfilment labour. Unlike domain trading, you can improve the product using engagement data. This makes it the strongest of your self-published digital-asset experiments.

However, 50 finished games are not 50 profitable games. Similar reskins can all fail for the same reason, so publishing quantity does not necessarily diversify risk. Choose three substantially different gameplay concepts first; concentrate further iteration on evidence of repeat play. Art consistency, onboarding, difficulty, mobile controls and performance still need human judgment and player testing when agents write all the code.

**Distribution and cash timing.** Playgama's official terms distinguish distribution channels: a 50% compensation rate applies on Playgama and its iframe partner network, while other partner-site revenue uses tiered rates. Its withdrawal threshold is $100. Do not apply the highest advertised share to all traffic. [Playgama payments](https://wiki.playgama.com/playgama/faq/payments-and-statistics)

CrazyGames provides another useful view of the commercial process: its Basic Launch tests engagement for 7–21 days, with ads disabled; strong performance can lead to Full Launch. Its published indicators include average playtime and next-day retention. These are platform-specific guideposts, not Playgama acceptance criteria or guarantees. [CrazyGames Basic Launch](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/basic-launch-metrics/)

CrazyGames also states NET 60 payment terms while currently aiming to pay sooner. A revenue dashboard and cash received in Finland can therefore differ materially within your deadline. [CrazyGames payouts](https://docs.crazygames.com/payouts/)

**Economics.** If your measured developer receipts are €5 per 1,000 sessions, after the portal share, €5,500 of receipts require **1.1 million sessions per month**; after €500 running costs, that leaves €5,000. At €2/session-RPM it takes 2.75 million sessions; at €10 it takes 550,000. These are sensitivity assumptions, not observed portal RPMs. Geography, session length, ad fill and ad frequency can change the result. If acquisition costs exceed net receipts per session, buying traffic does not solve the problem.

**Using it for paid client work.** A more controllable short-term offer is a branded browser mini-game for a marketing agency or business campaign, with one mechanic, clear mobile requirements, analytics, hosting and limited revisions. This is commissioned production, not passive game revenue or SaaS. An illustrative three projects/month at €2,500, with €500 external costs each and €500 fixed monthly costs, leaves €5,500 before your own labour and tax. The proposed price needs actual agency sales; scope and revisions decide whether the margin survives. Holiday campaigns could fit the coming September-to-January window, but that is an opportunity to test, not booked demand.

**SaaS assessment.** Selling the current studio to other solo game producers is a narrower and less proven opportunity than using it. Its main value is production governance; users still need coding, gameplay judgment and distribution. A B2B batch-production or publisher QA tool could eventually justify €99–299/month. A B2C “make money with AI games” subscription invites expectations you cannot substantiate. A broader hosted game builder would compete in another category and introduce untrusted-code execution, asset-rights and compute-cost exposure. Finishing it quickly would not prove demand.

For example, GDevelop already offers a free engine and paid plans combining AI, cloud projects and publishing. Its pricing page did not expose dependable paid amounts in this retrieval, so no numeric paid-price comparison is asserted. This is evidence of alternative capability, not a like-for-like replacement for Playforge's production workflow. [GDevelop plans](https://gdevelop.io/pricing)

**Rights are a per-product requirement.** The local ship gate only checks that license notes exist and gameplay/art conditions are recorded. That does not determine redistribution rights. Envato's license FAQ distinguishes an end product from the source item and generally treats new end products separately; an Extended License is not a general unlimited multi-client license. Do not infer a blanket ban on reskinning from the repository's wording either: check the actual license and intended distribution. [Envato license FAQ](https://codecanyon.net/licenses/faq)

**Commercial gate.** Ship three differentiated games early enough to observe real players. Scale a concept when engagement and net receipts improve under iteration. For the service route, require two paid agency/client projects and measure delivery hours and revision load. Do not build a 50-title factory before either route works.

**Verdict:** second overall. Best alternative if you prefer games and can sell commissioned work. Stronger own-output upside than Workforge, but portal income is less dependable within four months.

## 3. Omniforge — valuable production engine; needs a much narrower buyer

**What you have.** A Next.js/Payload application combining canonical book structure, derived editions, production graphs, generated artifacts, approval/lineage, ecommerce foundations, quote posters and social promotion. The new edition compiler is present in code even though its design document describes a plan: I treat the implementation as current. The registry marks ebook, quote-poster and social-promotion pipelines implemented; print books, audiobooks, illustrated/activity/puzzle books and other formats still contain explicit stubs. Under your assumption these can be completed within weeks; the commercial issue is which deserve completion.

Evidence: [format registry](C:/Dev/omniforge/src/lib/product-formats/registry.ts:16), [edition compiler](C:/Dev/omniforge/src/lib/product-formats/edition/compileEdition.ts:40), [EPUB content restrictions](C:/Dev/omniforge/src/lib/publishing/epub.ts:69), [Etsy connector](C:/Dev/omniforge/src/lib/producer/release/connectors/etsy.ts:198), [publishing-media access](C:/Dev/omniforge/src/collections/PublishingMedia.ts:16).

**Using it yourself.** The strongest offer is a specific, editorially useful product: a carefully adapted reading edition for a defined audience, or an original themed reading/learning package. Generic public-domain reprints and quote posters have weak differentiation. Cheaper production improves the margin on a sale; it does not establish readership. A popular Gutenberg download is evidence of interest in a free title, not evidence that people will buy your adaptation.

At €6 contribution per completed retail order, €500 fixed costs plus the €5,000 benchmark require **917 orders/month**. At €15 contribution, they require 367. Those contribution figures are assumptions after applicable platform fees, advertising, refunds and delivery costs; they cannot be inferred from list price. Measure profit by product and acquisition channel rather than number of generated books.

**Marketplace constraints.** KDP can refuse undifferentiated public-domain editions. Its accepted differentiation categories include original translation, annotation and relevant original illustration; formatting improvements alone are insufficient. Public-domain content also faces restrictions on royalty options and KDP Select. A modernized or simplified text should not automatically be assumed to satisfy those categories. [KDP public-domain policy](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200743940)

Etsy permits seller-prompted AI creations with disclosure, but excludes simple bundles/PDFs of somebody else's work from its “designed by a seller” category. Permission to reproduce a work and eligibility for a marketplace listing are different questions. [Etsy creativity standards](https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity/)

Project Gutenberg evaluates copyright primarily under US law; a title's US status does not establish its status in Finland or every destination market. Establish rights by edition, including translation and illustrations, before treating a large corpus as commercial inventory. [Project Gutenberg permissions](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/permission.html)

**Best B2B route.** Use the engine to deliver a reviewed EPUB plus an on-brand launch-content pack for small publishers or professional authors with an existing backlist. Work from customer-owned or authorized source material. Test a fixed €750–1,500 deliverable, with content volume and revisions specified. That is a service using Omniforge. Ongoing €199–499/month software becomes more credible when a publisher produces enough releases or backlist campaigns to need it repeatedly. General document ingestion and publisher workflow may require additions beyond the current Gutenberg-centered system.

**B2C SaaS route.** Weak if pitched as another formatter: Atticus advertises a $147 one-time purchase for ebook and print formatting. Omniforge needs to win on repeatable adaptation or coordinated production, not formatting alone. Source structure and provenance are potentially useful advantages, but the buyer has to value them in the finished workflow. [Atticus](https://www.atticus.io/)

**What must be finished.** For paid files, close the gap between generated artifacts and authorized delivery: publishing-media currently allows public reads and is stored under a public directory. The Etsy connector inspected creates/updates listings and uploads preview images, but does not establish a complete digital-file delivery and activation flow. Book formatting needs acceptance tests using difficult real manuscripts, especially footnotes and tables. Shared admin production needs customer separation and cost limits before being exposed as SaaS. None of these implies months of coding under your assumption; they define the commercial acceptance test.

**Commercial gate.** Three paid production customers buying the same deliverable, with repeat work from at least one, or ten retail products generating positive contribution through a measured acquisition channel. Do not expand every output format merely because the architecture can support it.

**Verdict:** third. Promising internal tooling for a focused publishing service. A broad autonomous content factory is a poor main-income bet for the next four months.

## 4. Vinted Bundles — quickest small cash experiment; weak main SaaS foundation

**What you have.** A specialized sourcing and resale workspace, not just a bundle-discount filter: seller/listing scans, saved filters, scheduled monitoring, multi-keyword searching, a deal workflow, favorites/storage and a browser extension. Its strongest commercial feature is finding several desirable items from one seller so discounts and combined shipping can improve unit economics. A discount percentage alone does not establish profit.

The extension also supports relisting, automatic offers and generated descriptions. Deal Finder expands discovery using listing popularity and search phrases. The comparison module takes a median of peer **asking prices**, with as few as two matching peers; the profit module applies a haircut and subtracts purchase and fixed shipping amounts. It is not verified sold-price research or complete resale accounting. Evidence: [comparable-listing selection](C:/Dev/vinted-bundles/lib/flipComps.js:49), [estimated-profit calculation](C:/Dev/vinted-bundles/lib/flipProfit.js:40), [Deal Finder pipeline](C:/Dev/vinted-bundles/lib/dealFinderPipeline.js:40), [extension features](C:/Dev/vinted-bundles/extension/README.md). A delegated review ran 73 targeted tests for pricing/comps, deal scoring, pipeline and selected extension behavior: all passed using test fixtures/mocks, without live marketplace actions. This verifies internal behavior, not live reliability or profitable resale.

**Using it yourself.** Focus on one category you can assess reliably, with enough value per item to justify handling, for example a narrow class of games or collectibles. Record landed purchase cost, condition/testing, likely selling venue, realized price, fees, postage, returns and minutes worked. Asking-price comparisons can guide investigation; completed paid sales establish the economics. Inventory that remains unsold is tied-up cash, not earned margin.

At €15 realized contribution per item, covering €500 monthly overhead plus €5,000 requires **367 sold items/month**. At €30 contribution it takes 184. If each item consumes 25 minutes across sourcing, testing, listing, messages and packing, those volumes imply about 153 or 77 hours respectively, before additional sourcing failures and administration. These are illustrative assumptions; the higher-margin case could support a living, but requires sourcing depth and turnover, not simply faster scans. Agents cannot inspect physical condition or pack parcels.

**B2C SaaS.** Casual bargain hunters have limited recurring willingness to pay. Professional resellers are better prospects because time saved and profit can be measured, but a sourcing subscription should not promise margins from listing-price estimates. Alerts are time-sensitive: selling identical scarce opportunities to many users makes them compete for the same item and erodes the advantage. This puts a practical limit on monetizing your own sourcing edge as a shared feed.

**B2B SaaS.** A reseller workflow with landed-cost accounting, stock aging, realized margin and permitted integrations is more defensible than broad automated scanning. That would be a deliberate extension of the current project. A €19–49/month extension or local tool could have buyers, but distribution, installation support and upstream changes remain substantial. Hosting it does not create permission to automate the underlying marketplace.

There is already low-price competition. VintedCRM advertises an extension at €12.99/month excluding VAT and a currently discounted broader Pro plan at €29/month excluding VAT, with its extension add-on priced separately. Your €19–49 hypothesis therefore needs a specific advantage beyond basic relisting and offers. [VintedCRM prices](https://vintedcrm.com/tarifs/)

**Architecture and platform constraints.** The application uses shared local/server storage and a global scan lock rather than isolated customer accounts and independent worker capacity. Scheduling and seller enrichment multiply requests. These are fixable technical issues, but more throughput may amplify the platform problem. The repository README's description of an “open public API” should not be treated as an official commercial API grant. Check current Vinted automation terms and commercial-selling eligibility in Finland before relying on systematic resale on Vinted or selling the automation to others. A browser extension is not inherently exempt from the same rules.

The current Finnish terms prohibit unauthorized external bots/scraping and commercialization of site content without consent; they also prohibit repeated deletion/relisting and bulk relisting. Ordinary membership is described as personal use, subject to the Pro exception. These restrictions overlap actual project features, so they are central business dependencies. [Vinted Finland terms, sections 1 and 6](https://www.vinted.fi/terms-and-conditions)

Finland is absent from Vinted's published Pro eligibility list, although the help page allows that tests in other countries may exist. Therefore a Finland-based professional resale business should not assume it can obtain Pro access. An official Pro API does exist, but access is restricted to allowlisted businesses and covers workflows such as their inventory and orders; it is not general authorization to scrape the market. [Vinted Pro eligibility](https://www.vinted.com/help/392/918-vinted-pro-service), [official Pro integrations](https://pro-docs.svc.vinted.com/)

Before any public hosting, fix the unauthenticated settings endpoint returning proxy/LLM credentials and the permissive CORS policy. This was identified in code; no credential values were extracted for the report. Evidence: [server settings endpoint](C:/Dev/vinted-bundles/server.js:408), [CORS configuration](C:/Dev/vinted-bundles/server.js:389), [global scan lock](C:/Dev/vinted-bundles/lib/scanLock.js:19). This is a required completion task, not a reason to assume lengthy development.

**Commercial gate.** A small, separately recorded batch of inventory with actual completed sales. Continue only if realized hourly earnings, return rate and stock turnover support expansion. Use an authorized platform/workflow; do not fund a rollout whose essential access rights remain unresolved. Record platform terms separately from ordinary technical reliability.

**Verdict:** fourth as a primary business; potentially first for learning quickly whether your personal sourcing expertise can earn cash. It is principally a resale operation, with software improving one part of it.

## 5. Domainforge — useful specialist tooling; worst match for the income deadline

**What you have.** Substantial domain operations software: closeout/drop feeds, several name-generation styles, lexicon and brand filters, availability/RDAP/Wayback/backlink enrichment, human review, registrar purchase routing, backorders/sniping, inventory and sale-status polling. It is primarily a local single-operator system, with a Streamlit interface and shared settings, not an existing multi-tenant SaaS.

Evidence: [inventory model](C:/Dev/domainforge/backend/app/models/inventory.py:12), [purchase routing](C:/Dev/domainforge/backend/app/services/purchase/router.py:256), [listing-status polling](C:/Dev/domainforge/backend/app/services/listings/job.py:1), [assumed price bands](C:/Dev/domainforge/backend/app/services/valuation/buy_score.py:18), [buy-score calculation](C:/Dev/domainforge/backend/app/services/valuation/buy_score.py:137).

**Most consequential finding.** The buy-score calculation maps an LLM score to €199/€249/€299 listing prices, deducts assumed costs and weights a score. It does not model the probability of sale or time to sale. Its “profit-aware” label therefore means conditional margin if a sale happens, not expected profit. Backlink or name-quality scores are useful filters but are not liquidity evidence. Sale-status polling improves bookkeeping; it does not supply buyers.

**Using it yourself.** At the code's illustrative €199 price, 18% fee and €12 acquisition cost, a completed sale contributes €151.18 before renewal costs and other overhead. Covering €500 of overhead plus €5,000 requires **37 completed sales every month**. The software can help register 37 domains much more easily than it can find 37 paying buyers.

For scale sensitivity, suppose 1,000 held domains have a hypothetical 2% annual sell-through rate, a €199 average realized price, an 18% fee and €12/year holding cost per domain. Annual proceeds after fees are about €3,264 against €12,000 of holding costs, before tools and replacements. Even at a hypothetical 5% sell-through, proceeds are about €8,159. These rates are scenarios, not asserted industry averages or estimates of your portfolio. The point is that holding cost and sale probability can reverse a seemingly attractive per-sale margin.

Current outside checks reinforce the need to use actual inputs: Afternic's public rates vary by plan/nameserver setup, and Dynadot lists standard .com renewal at $10.88, with premium domains treated differently. The code's 18% and €12 are not universal fees. [Afternic selling fees](https://www.afternic.com/sell-domains), [Dynadot .com prices](https://www.dynadot.com/domain/com)

**SaaS assessment.** The most plausible buyer is an active domain investor who wants workflow consolidation, personalized watchlists, historical evidence and portfolio controls. There is competition for this budget: SpamZilla advertises $37/month for domain research. Discovery alone is not a differentiated paid offer. A €29–79/month bring-your-own-registrar-key product is a hypothesis worth testing later, with spending limits and careful credential isolation. [SpamZilla pricing](https://www.spamzilla.io/pricing/)

Selling the same scarce purchase recommendations to many buyers also creates conflicts, especially if you buy the best names yourself. A paid shortlist tailored to each buyer is more credible, but becomes research/service work. A simple startup name generator is low-frequency B2C demand; operating a domain marketplace would require liquidity on both sides and is a different business.

**What would change the assessment.** A verified profitable portfolio with acquisition cohorts, time-to-sale, net proceeds and renewal costs, or several active investors paying for the workflow. In their absence, capital turnover is the limiting factor even with flawless automation. Maintain a capped experiment; do not count inventory value, automated appraisals or occasional sales as predictable living expenses.

**Verdict:** fifth for the 3–4 month horizon. It may be worth keeping as your specialist tool, but the deadline favors businesses where you can directly sell a deliverable or subscription.

## The next 16 weeks

**Weeks 1–2: sell the Workforge outcome while agents finish it.** Choose small private coaching/outplacement teams as the first segment. Build one excellent demo using authorized sample data: their current template, a job description, the generated application pack and a visible factual review. Create a clear paid pilot offer with volume, review responsibility and price. Prepare 50 named prospects and seek 10 substantive buyer conversations. These are activities for you to perform; no outreach was sent during this assessment.

**Weeks 3–4: obtain and deliver three paid pilots.** An introductory €250–500 pilot can test payment intent; it is not recurring revenue. Measure current versus assisted preparation time, factual errors, revision requests, support minutes and API cost. Finish only features necessary to deliver the agreed workflow. Avoid making employer-ranking, job guarantees or automated applications part of the offer.

**Weeks 5–8: demonstrate renewal.** Seek five to eight paying organizations and renewals from the initial cohort, at the actual ongoing price. Add the small set of shared features buyers repeatedly request. Build a customer-approved demonstration of time saved. A list of complimentary pilots is insufficient evidence.

**Weeks 9–12: repeat acquisition.** Aim toward roughly 10–15 appropriately priced organizations. Prioritize referrals and partnerships among providers serving the same audience. Keep source feeds optional and document workflows valuable without LinkedIn. Record owner time honestly; software revenue that requires bespoke full-time work per customer will not scale.

**Weeks 13–16: judge the business on collected recurring revenue and workload.** At the modeled €499 price, around 13 accounts reach the €5,000 operating-surplus example. If the achieved price is lower, the required customer count rises. Check renewals, customer concentration, arrears and realistic support capacity before treating the income as established.

**Stop or redirect rules.** If 10–15 qualified demonstrations with a working product produce no paid pilots, revisit the buyer and offer instead of adding features. If pilots pay but do not renew, identify whether the failure is output quality, insufficient time saved or episodic demand. By week eight, if there is still no repeat purchase, move the main commercial effort to Playforge commissioned games or Omniforge paid publishing production, whichever has secured an actual paid order. An order is stronger evidence than this desk-research ranking.

**Allocation:** put the large majority of business attention into Workforge for the first eight weeks. Keep Playforge as the named alternative, rather than actively growing five products. Preserve the other repositories and cap their experiments. Under the assumed few-week coding timeline, attention spent acquiring and retaining buyers has more influence on the December/January outcome than parallel feature completion.

## Confidence and what could reverse the ranking

Confidence is high in the distinction between built functionality and paid demand, and in the cited platform constraints.
